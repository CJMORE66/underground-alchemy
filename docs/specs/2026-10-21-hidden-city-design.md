# CAISIK: The Hidden City

The user's supplied master prompt is the approved implementation scope: expand the existing public Site into an underground cultural atlas, retaining its identity, artwork archive, collection pages and store destination.

The chosen design is an obsidian and aged-gold editorial atlas. A large typographic opening leads to a geographic Paris explorer, seven interactive philosophical studies, a practical freestyle laboratory, eight original visual compositions and a private browser notebook. Real photographs illustrate real places; artwork is identified as artwork. Documentary evidence, reception history, contemporary interpretation and reflection are visibly distinct.

Keep the existing static deployment and local fonts. Build a new homepage using small ES modules and a bundled map library. Store only explicit saved places, routes, learning progress and optional notes in this browser. Microphone capture requires a button press, remains in memory and offers playback, download and deletion. Geolocation is requested only on demand; precise coordinates are never saved or placed in third-party requests.

Paris is the only live city; future city expansion uses a city field in the dataset. Include ten or more source-backed profiles and three ordered thematic routes. Filters cover topic, period, accessibility and saved places. Unknown accessibility is not advertised as confirmed step-free. Every profile links to official access information and explains time-sensitive limitations. Le MUR is a curated commissioned wall, not permission for visitors to paint.

The Kybalion is attributed to Three Initiates, 1908, and distinguished from ancient Hermetica and experimental science. All seven principles get original diagrams, a manipulation, urban analogy, reflective and lyrical exercise. Learning has no arbitrary locks: completing a reflection reveals a contextual connection.

Completion requires observed browser journeys, automated checks for data/filter/storage behavior, independent review, unchanged Site identity/audience, saved source provenance and successful publication.
